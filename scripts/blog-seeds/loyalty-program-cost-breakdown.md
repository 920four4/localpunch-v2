"How much does a loyalty program cost?" is the right question — and most answers online are useless because they hide fees behind "contact sales."

Here's what small-business loyalty actually costs in 2026, with real numbers you can plan around.

## Tier 1: Paper punch cards (~$50/year)

**What you pay:** Printed cards + a stamper. Call it $50 one-time, maybe $20/year in reprints.

**What you don't get:** A customer list, fraud protection, or completion rates above ~30% by punch 10 (industry ballpark).

Paper is cheapest upfront and most expensive if you're trying to grow.

## Tier 2: DIY QR + spreadsheet ($0–$100)

Some owners try Google Forms, manual tallies, or static QR codes.

**Hidden costs:** Staff time, mistakes, zero anti-fraud, and customers who screenshot a QR and punch from home.

DIY looks free until you count an hour a week of owner labor.

## Tier 3: Loyalty software ($20–$200+/month)

Typical models:

- **Per location** — hurts if you expand
- **Per customer or visit** — hurts when you're busy
- **Feature tiers** — you pay for CRM you never open

LocalPunch is **$60/month or $600/year** flat: unlimited programs, customers, punches, redemptions. No per-visit fee.

## What "unlimited" is worth

If you punch 400 times a month and a competitor charges per visit, your bill scales with success. Flat pricing means a good month doesn't punish you.

## ROI back-of-napkin

Assume digital doubles completed reward cycles vs paper (common pattern when cards aren't lost).

- 100 active customers
- 3 extra visits/year after completing a card
- $8 average ticket
- **~$2,400/year** incremental revenue

Subtract $720/year software → still positive for most shops doing more than a handful of regulars.

## Questions to ask any vendor

1. Is pricing flat or does it scale with visits?
2. Do customers need to download an app?
3. Do I get a contact list I can text?
4. How long is setup realistically?
5. Can I cancel without a phone call?

## Bottom line

The cheapest program on paper is often the most expensive in lost regulars. The right question isn't "what's the monthly fee" — it's **"what does a completed loyalty cycle return?"**
